Interdisciplinary · Virtual exchange

A shared first-year course across two countries

I designed a shared first-year course that linked political science students in Japan with sociology students in Maine, and I wrote its website.

My role
Designed the exchange. Wrote the website code and taught students to code. Co-taught with Dr. Kiyohiko Toyama and Dr. Cindy Lahar.

The challenge

The political science students at Miyazaki International College (now Miyazaki International University) were not familiar with American culture of the Northeast, while the sociology students at York County Technical College (now York County Community College) were largely unfamiliar with Japanese culture at large. These differences created a natural foundation for cross-cultural exchange and gave students from both disciplines a meaningful reason to collaborate, learn from one another, and explore social and political issues from different cultural perspectives.

The approach

Both classes shared one website. Students researched political and social issues together. They chatted weekly (socially), discussed course content in writing, and presented their findings in written, spoken and video formats.

I wrote the website code. I also taught students to code, so they could publish their own work.

Why it matters todayThis approach is now called virtual exchange, or COIL (Collaborative Online International Learning).

The team

I co-taught with Dr. Kiyohiko Toyama (political science) and Dr. Cindy Lahar (sociology, Maine).

The outcome

The course led to a peer-reviewed paper and two conference papers:

Maggard, J. (2002). Computer-mediated communication and political science: A collaborative learning approach. Comparative Culture, 8, 119–129.

Interlanguage development through collaborative learning with CMC. Paper presented at the Integrating Content and Language: Meeting the Challenge of a Multilingual Higher Education Conference, Universiteit Maastricht, Netherlands, October 2003.

Creating iMovie documentaries instead of term papers. Paper presented (with Elizabeth Lokon) at the TESOL International Convention, Long Beach, USA, April 2004.

Tools I used

  • HTML and CSS: I wrote the shared course website by hand and taught students to code their own pages.
