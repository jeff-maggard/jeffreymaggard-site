Embedded Calibrated Grading and Automated Writing Moderation System

I built a Microsoft 365 system that automates writing moderation (grading calibration) for exams, with a Rise 360 orientation for lecturers.

The purpose

At every midterm and final exam, lecturers mark writing samples in a moderation session, so that grading is consistent and fair across each course. Each course used to have a co-course lead who ran these sessions. When that role was removed, the work fell on the course leads, or on a faculty member they assigned.

Done by hand, it takes about 20 hours per course lead each semester: sending emails, collecting scores, analyzing them, sending feedback to every lecturer and keeping records. I built this system to do all of that automatically.

Every session is archived, so the department builds a record of marking accuracy over time. That evidence supports data-driven review: we can see where calibration drifts, target training where it is needed, and reflect on and improve our assessment practice.

What the course lead does

  1. Choose the midterm or final exam.
  2. Add three anonymized samples of student writing.
  3. Set the opening and closing dates.

That is the whole setup. Everything else runs on its own.

What happens automatically

  1. Power Automate emails every lecturer when the session opens.
  2. Lecturers score the samples in one Microsoft Form.
  3. When the session closes, Excel with Office Scripts compares each score with the key mark. The course lead chooses the key mark: their own scores, or the median of the lecturers who marked the sample.
  4. Each lecturer gets one email showing their mark for each rubric criterion directly above the key mark.
  5. A lecturer more than 0.5 from the key mark marks another sample and gets instant feedback. For example, if the median is 3 and a lecturer gives a 4 or a 2, they mark another sample. They get up to two extra samples.
  6. If they are still out of range, the system emails the course lead, so they can meet and help.
  7. SharePoint archives all the data, and the course leads receive a report. A name-free summary can go to the department for CEA accreditation.

Why it matters

  • Saves each course lead about 20 hours a semester.
  • Frees the faculty member who was assigned to run moderation for other work that builds the program.
  • Shows course leads which lecturers need support, so they can help them early.
  • Provides documented evidence of assessment quality for CEA accreditation.

Orientation

I made a 5-minute Rise 360 orientation for lecturers, published as SCORM 2004 in Blackboard Ultra.

Next steps

Built and tested with invented data in November 2026. With permission from the department, I will pilot it in Spring 2027 with the Embedded Foundation courses. If it works well, it can expand to the Foundation Program and Post-Foundation courses, so all three divisions of the department can use it.
