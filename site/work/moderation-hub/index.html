The Moderation Hub

I designed and built a Microsoft 365 system that automates writing moderation (grading calibration) for exams, with a Rise 360 orientation for lecturers.

On this page

  2. How it works
  3. Orientation

Why I built it

Moderation sessions keep writing grades fair: every lecturer marks the same samples, and the marks are compared. Running them by hand takes course leads many hours each midterm and final exam.

I built the Moderation Hub to automate every step. The course lead only scans in three samples and sets the opening and closing dates. The rest is automatic.

How it works

  1. The course lead adds three anonymized writing samples and sets the opening and closing dates.
  2. The Hub emails every lecturer, who scores the samples in one Microsoft Form.
  3. When the session closes, the Hub analyzes the scores. Each lecturer gets one email that shows their mark for each rubric criterion directly above the key mark. The course lead chooses the key mark: their own scores, or the median of the lecturers who marked the sample.
  4. A lecturer whose score is more than 0.5 from the key mark marks another sample. For example, if the median is 3 and a lecturer gives a 4 or a 2, they mark another sample and get instant feedback.
  5. If they are still out of range, they get another sample. If they miss a third time, the Hub emails the course lead, so they can meet and help.
  6. At the end of the session, the Hub archives all the data and sends a report to the course leads. A name-free summary can go to the department for accreditation.

Orientation

I made a 5-minute Rise 360 orientation for lecturers, published as SCORM 2004 in Blackboard Ultra.

Status

Built and tested with invented data in November 2026. A pilot is planned for Spring 2027.

Tools I used

  • Microsoft Forms: lecturers score the writing samples.
  • Excel and Office Scripts: the scores are analyzed against the key mark.
  • Power Automate: every email, extra sample and alert is sent automatically.
  • SharePoint: samples, results and the semester archive are stored.
  • Articulate Rise 360: the 5-minute orientation, published as SCORM 2004 in Blackboard Ultra.
  • Claude: AI-assisted design and build, with documented human review.
