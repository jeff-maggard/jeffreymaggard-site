The purpose

To keep a Qatari folktale alive and share its lesson with new audiences: first in a book, then in a museum exhibition, and now as an animated film.

Watch

The story

“He Who Doesn’t Know the Falcon Roasts It” is one of the 19 stories in Tales of Qatari Heritage, the bilingual book I lead as Managing Editor. Qatar University students on my Cultural Awareness Team collected it from their elders. I edited it with a team of faculty editors and translators: Alaeddin Salah Halwani, Charles Fullerton, Daniela Slaveva Tzenkova, David Paul Finfrock, Dhafir Kasassbeh, Dr. Ali Ahmed Hussein AlGaali, Dr. Karima Ben Abbes, Dr. Magdi Ahmed Kandil, Dr. Mondher Chaabane, Elisabeth Anna Szewczyk, Hayat El Samad, Manal Bassyony-Cooper, Roy Randy Y. Briones and Zadjia Zahi.

This story concludes Lila Abu-Lughod’s opening chapter in On the Move: Reframing Nomadic Pastoralism (Doha: Qatar Museums; [Milan]: Skira, 2022). The National Museum of Qatar featured the story in its On the Move exhibition (Fall 2022 – Winter 2023). The book acknowledges me and my Qatar University faculty editorial team.

From story to screen

AnimationFrom story to screenStoryboards, a locked Character Guide and AI animation for my folktale series.

I am writing and producing an animated series of folktales from Qatar, UAE, Mongolia, and Japan. Below you can see how I approach the design elements and characters.

  • Look: 2D animation, Pixar-inspired. Clean shapes, expressive faces and cinematic light.
  • Setting: an authentic Qatari desert and culture.
  • Rules: a written style guide, cast guide, cultural-accuracy rules and a QA checklist for every scene.
  • Sound: story subtitles, voice and lip sync, and an original music theme.
